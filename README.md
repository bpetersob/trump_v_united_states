## The Founding Fathers on Presidential Immunity: A Reaction to the Supreme Court's 2024 Ruling

**On July 1, 2024, the Supreme Court of the United States, in the landmark case of *Trump v. United States*, fundamentally altered the landscape of presidential accountability [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States)[[2]](https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/breaking-down-the-trump-immunity-decision) . In a 6-3 decision, the Court ruled that former presidents possess a degree of immunity from criminal prosecution for official acts performed while in office [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[4]](https://ballotpedia.org/Trump_v._United_States)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States) . The majority opinion, authored by Chief Justice John Roberts, rejected both the idea of absolute immunity and the argument for no immunity, instead creating a new, nuanced framework for lower courts to apply [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States) . The dissenting justices argued this was a stark departure from the original constitutional design [[5]](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-939_e2pg.pdf)[[6]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/read-the-full-supreme-court-decision-on-trump-and-presidential-immunity)[[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939) .**

This report channels the voices of George Washington, Thomas Jefferson, John Adams, James Madison, and Alexander Hamilton, imagining their responses to this momentous decision. Their reactions are not to a simple declaration that the president is "above the law," but to the specific, three-tiered structure the Court created:

*   **Absolute Immunity** for acts within a president's core constitutional powers (e.g., pardons, vetoes) [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States)[[7]](https://pennlawreview.com/2026/03/06/power-and-immunity-in-youngstown-and-trump-v-united-states/) .
*   **Presumptive Immunity** for all other official acts, which prosecutors must overcome [[4]](https://ballotpedia.org/Trump_v._United_States)[[8]](https://www.law.virginia.edu/node/2202121) .
*   **No Immunity** for purely private or unofficial conduct [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[2]](https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/breaking-down-the-trump-immunity-decision) .

How would the architects of the nation react to a ruling that carves out spheres of legal protection for the very office they designed to be subordinate to the Constitution? Each reaction below is grounded in their documented philosophies, political writings, and personal correspondence, providing a glimpse into the constitutional crisis they might perceive.

### George Washington: A Dangerous Step Toward Monarchy

"It is with a grave and heavy heart that I absorb this Court's decree, which, while professing deference to the law, has nonetheless placed the President in a special class above his fellow citizens [[9]](https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1197&context=ljh)[[10]](https://truthout.org/articles/no-one-should-be-above-the-law/) . I did not fight George III to become George I [[11]](https://www.quora.com/Did-George-Washington-see-himself-as-President-or-King-and-which-did-he-prefer-for-the-future-of-America) . My every action, from insisting on the simple title 'Mr. President' to voluntarily relinquishing power, was to establish that the executive is a servant of the people, not their master [[12]](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/the-first-president/the-farewell-address)[[13]](https://www.mountvernon.org/library/digitalhistory/digital-encyclopedia/article/presidential-precedents)[[14]](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/first-american-president-setting-precedent) . This ruling, by granting 'absolute immunity' for what it deems 'core' powers [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States)[[7]](https://pennlawreview.com/2026/03/06/power-and-immunity-in-youngstown-and-trump-v-united-states/) , creates a shield behind which the very 'cunning, ambitious, and unprincipled men' I warned against may 'usurp for themselves the reins of government' [[12]](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/the-first-president/the-farewell-address)[[15]](https://www.mountvernon.org/library/digitalhistory/past-projects/quotes/article/however-political-parties-may-now-and-then-answer-popular-ends-they-are-likely-in-the-course-of-time-and-things-to-become-potent-engines-by-which-cunning-ambitious-and-unprincipled-men-will-be-enabled-to-subvert-the-power-of-the-people-and-to-usurp-for-th) . To declare any official act entirely beyond the reach of criminal law is to invite the 'spirit of encroachment' that consolidates power and creates 'a real despotism' [[16]](https://oll.libertyfund.org/quotes/george-washington-warns-the-nation-in-his-farewell-address-that-love-of-power-will-tend-to-create-a-real-despotism-in-america-unless-proper-checks-and-balances-are-maintained-to-limit-government-power-1796) . We did not cast off a king only to see the judiciary fashion a new one, cloaked in the robes of 'official acts.' This is a betrayal of the principle that the Constitution is 'sacredly obligatory upon all' [[17]](https://www.nationalreview.com/corner/george-washington-on-respecting-the-constitution-as-written/)[[18]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/washing.asp) ."

**Justification for Washington's Perspective:**

George Washington was acutely aware of his role in setting precedents for a law-abiding executive, meticulously working to distinguish the presidency from a monarchy [[19]](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/two-georges/about-this-exhibition/president-and-king/)[[20]](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/the-first-president/a-president-by-any-other-name) . His famous declaration, "I didn't fight George III to become George I," guided his conduct [[11]](https://www.quora.com/Did-George-Washington-see-himself-as-President-or-King-and-which-did-he-prefer-for-the-future-of-America) . He rejected grandiose titles for the simple "Mr. President" and, most critically, his voluntary retirement after two terms was a deliberate act to establish that the American executive is not a ruler for life [[12]](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/the-first-president/the-farewell-address)[[21]](https://www.facebook.com/newshour/posts/george-washington-had-no-desire-to-become-the-democratic-equivalent-of-a-monarch/1490024959659412/)[[14]](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/first-american-president-setting-precedent)[[22]](https://itrfoundation.org/remembering-the-legacy-of-george-washington/) . He would view the creation of a category of "absolute immunity" [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States)[[7]](https://pennlawreview.com/2026/03/06/power-and-immunity-in-youngstown-and-trump-v-united-states/)  as a direct assault on these principles, creating an unaccountable leader akin to the monarch he fought to overthrow [[23]](https://www.cato.org/blog/george-washingtons-legacy-electoral-count-reform-act)[[24]](https://www.smithscastle.org/the-greatest-mischiefthat-would-befall-my-country-washington-refuses-to-be-king-by-r/) .

His Farewell Address delivered a forceful warning against the dangers of unchecked power, which he saw as a direct threat to liberty [[12]](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/the-first-president/the-farewell-address)[[16]](https://oll.libertyfund.org/quotes/george-washington-warns-the-nation-in-his-farewell-address-that-love-of-power-will-tend-to-create-a-real-despotism-in-america-unless-proper-checks-and-balances-are-maintained-to-limit-government-power-1796) . He cautioned against the "spirit of encroachment" that leads to despotism and identified the human "love of power, and proneness to abuse it" as a fundamental threat requiring robust checks and balances [[16]](https://oll.libertyfund.org/quotes/george-washington-warns-the-nation-in-his-farewell-address-that-love-of-power-will-tend-to-create-a-real-despotism-in-america-unless-proper-checks-and-balances-are-maintained-to-limit-government-power-1796) . He feared that political factions would become engines for "cunning, ambitious, and unprincipled men" to subvert the people's power, leading to a "frightful despotism" [[12]](https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/the-first-president/the-farewell-address)[[25]](https://www.americanyawp.com/reader/a-new-nation/george-washington-farewell-address-1796/)[[18]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/washing.asp) . A ruling that shields a president's official acts from prosecution would, in his eyes, be a tool for such men.

For Washington, the rule of law was the "firmest pillar of good Government" [[26]](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mgw2.022/?sp=177&st=text)[[27]](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/proclamation-7670-law-day-usa-2003) . He asserted that the Constitution was "sacredly obligatory upon all" and that it is the "duty of every individual to obey the established government" [[17]](https://www.nationalreview.com/corner/george-washington-on-respecting-the-constitution-as-written/)[[18]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/washing.asp) . His decisive action to suppress the Whiskey Rebellion, taken at the urging of his Treasury Secretary, demonstrated his unwavering commitment to enforcing the law upon all citizens [[28]](https://lawandhistoryreview.org/article/george-washingtons-constitutional-theory/)[[29]](https://www.exploros.com/summary/The-Whiskey-Rebellion-Testing-Federal-Authority-2)[[30]](https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/teachers/lesson_plans/pdfs/unit3_5.pdf)[[31]](https://www.ttb.gov/public-information/special-feature) . The idea that a president could not be criminally prosecuted for a corrupt use of a "core" power would be seen as undoing his careful work to establish an accountable executive, violating the bedrock principle that no official is above the law [[9]](https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1197&context=ljh)[[10]](https://truthout.org/articles/no-one-should-be-above-the-law/) .

### Thomas Jefferson: The Judiciary Forges New Chains

"I can scarcely express the horror I feel, though I cannot claim to feel surprise . I have long warned that the judiciary is a 'despotic branch,' working like gravity to advance its 'noiseless step like a thief, over the field of jurisdiction' [[32]](https://www.facebook.com/groups/339317480411211/posts/1838450463831231/) . Now, this 'subtle corps of sappers and miners' has used its un-elected power not only to place itself above the other branches, but to shield the executive from the law as well [[33]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1CGisLAAYwnlQ134sUukRLhNGCAIVxvGO) . They have made the Constitution 'a mere thing of wax' to be twisted into any shape they please [[34]](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/letter-from-thomas-jefferson-to-spencer-roane-september-6-1819/) . The Court claims it must prevent a 'chilled' executive [[35]](https://journals.law.harvard.edu/jlpp/the-presidential-immunity-decision-robert-delahunty-john-yoo/)[[5]](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-939_e2pg.pdf) , but this is a preposterous assault on republican virtue. A public servant *should* be chilled by the prospect of breaking the law! When I pursued the prosecution of my own former Vice President, Aaron Burr, for treason, it was from the conviction that no man, regardless of station, is above the law [[36]](https://irlaw.umkc.edu/context/faculty_works/article/1848/viewcontent/The_Treason_Trial_of_Aaron_Burr.pdf)[[37]](https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/the-great-trial-that-tested-the-constitutions-treason-clause)[[38]](https://www.britannica.com/today-in-history/May-22-Aaron-Burrs-Treason-Trial-Begins) . Even when I myself acted beyond the enumerated powers to purchase the Louisiana territory, I did so with the full understanding that an executive must 'throw himself on the justice of his country' [[39]](https://www.americanyawp.com/text/07-the-early-republic/)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase)[[41]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1QoQPtsEWL1-o3Yi430myawIFC3muPEXGKg6ElbMCs8U) . This ruling does the opposite; it removes that risk and in so doing, dissolves accountability. The 'despotism of an oligarchy' I so feared has arrived, and it has come to protect the very 'elective despotism' I warned against [[42]](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/letter-to-william-torrance/)[[43]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1P4ZpJ5pi4t0L9xO162vrx6Qw2tLbj1lSJ7-b1IISsVs)[[44]](https://press-pubs.uchicago.edu/founders/documents/v1ch10s9.html) ."

**Justification for Jefferson's Perspective:**

Thomas Jefferson’s entire political philosophy was animated by a profound fear of centralized power, which he believed was the natural enemy of liberty [[45]](https://millercenter.org/president/jefferson/domestic-affairs)[[43]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1P4ZpJ5pi4t0L9xO162vrx6Qw2tLbj1lSJ7-b1IISsVs) . He warned against an "elective despotism" where all powers might be concentrated in one branch, even an elected one [[46]](https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/historic-document-library/detail/thomas-jefferson-notes-on-the-state-of-virginia-1782)[[47]](https://www.azquotes.com/quote/570550) . This ruling would be, in his eyes, the fulfillment of his darkest prophecies.

*   **Distrust of the Judiciary:** Jefferson's fear of the federal judiciary was even more pronounced than his concern for the executive [[48]](https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/disp_textbook.cfm?smtID=2&psid=2982) . He viewed life-appointed judges as an unaccountable "despotic branch" working to undermine the republic [[49]](https://cooperative-individualism.org/jefferson-thomas_correspondence-constitution-united-states-separation-of-powers-1820.htm)[[32]](https://www.facebook.com/groups/339317480411211/posts/1838450463831231/)[[33]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1CGisLAAYwnlQ134sUukRLhNGCAIVxvGO) . He was a staunch opponent of judicial review, believing it would lead to the "despotism of an oligarchy" and make the Constitution "a mere thing of wax in the hands of the judiciary" [[34]](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/letter-from-thomas-jefferson-to-spencer-roane-september-6-1819/)[[42]](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/letter-to-william-torrance/)[[50]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1DJzZVFuyZExkY-IlW3eUqNs61Kspke3u) . He would see this ruling as the ultimate act of judicial overreach: the branch he trusted least, using its power to shield the branch he feared most.

*   **Accountability in Practice (The Burr Trial):** Jefferson's actions during the 1807 treason trial of his former Vice President, Aaron Burr, provide a stark illustration of his belief in holding high-ranking officials criminally accountable [[36]](https://irlaw.umkc.edu/context/faculty_works/article/1848/viewcontent/The_Treason_Trial_of_Aaron_Burr.pdf)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burr_conspiracy)[[38]](https://www.britannica.com/today-in-history/May-22-Aaron-Burrs-Treason-Trial-Begins) . Convinced of Burr's plot to separate western states, Jefferson aggressively managed the prosecution, provided documents, and publicly declared Burr's "guilt is placed beyond all question" before a grand jury had even returned an indictment [[52]](https://www.neh.gov/humanities/2013/mayjune/feature/burr-versus-jefferson-versus-marshall)[[53]](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-speeches/january-22-1807-special-message-congress-burr-conspiracy)[[54]](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/thomas-jefferson-aaron-burr-and-american-way-treason-180962573/)[[37]](https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/the-great-trial-that-tested-the-constitutions-treason-clause) . When Chief Justice John Marshall issued a subpoena for presidential papers, Jefferson asserted a form of executive privilege, arguing the president must have discretion over what to disclose [[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burr_conspiracy)[[55]](https://lawcat.berkeley.edu/record/1116432/files/fulltext.pdf) . However, he did not defy the court, instead "substantially" complying by providing some of the requested documents [[56]](https://www.fjc.gov/sites/default/files/trials/burrtrial.pdf) . This complex episode shows that while Jefferson believed in protecting executive confidentiality, he never asserted total immunity from the judicial process itself and actively used that process against a high-ranking official [[57]](https://www.americanacorner.com/blog/burr-treason-trial)[[52]](https://www.neh.gov/humanities/2013/mayjune/feature/burr-versus-jefferson-versus-marshall) .

*   **Accountability in Principle (The Louisiana Purchase):** While Jefferson feared an "elective despotism," his own presidency tested his strict constructionist philosophy [[58]](https://www.bu.edu/law/journals-archive/bulr/documents/yoo.pdf)[[59]](https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/the-louisiana-purchase-jeffersons-constitutional-gamble) . The Louisiana Purchase of 1803 presented a monumental opportunity, but the Constitution did not explicitly grant the president power to acquire foreign territory [[60]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/e-lessons/jefferson-and-the-louisiana-purchase/)[[61]](https://americainclass.org/jefferson-and-the-louisiana-purchase/)[[62]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1h04pGUzJvucG5g22329knqXN28TwSTDmo4Gf__8x0cg) . Jefferson acknowledged he was stretching his authority beyond its enumerated powers [[60]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/e-lessons/jefferson-and-the-louisiana-purchase/)[[63]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1DjBCmU8fTyg5c9o8AAIUJNaCKvKpqaNIAX8RJAeAM24) . However, he did not believe this action should be without consequence or review. He argued that an executive who acts beyond the law for the nation's good must then "throw himself on the justice of his country" [[39]](https://www.americanyawp.com/text/07-the-early-republic/)[[41]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1QoQPtsEWL1-o3Yi430myawIFC3muPEXGKg6ElbMCs8U) . This concept is a direct refutation of immunity. For Jefferson, the willingness to face personal legal and political consequences was a cornerstone of republican governance, and a ruling that removes this risk would be anathema to him.

### John Adams: A Government of Categories, Not of Laws

"This is not a ruling; it is a demolition. It is the destruction of the very foundation upon which we sought to build this new world: 'a government of laws, and not of men' [[64]](https://constitutioncenter.org/education/classroom-resource-library/classroom/6.5-primary-source-john-adams-thoughts-on-government-1776) . In the Massachusetts Constitution, I labored to erect 'a government of laws, and not of men' by meticulously separating the powers of government [[65]](https://www.mass.gov/guides/john-adams-the-massachusetts-constitution)[[66]](https://allthingsliberty.com/2022/05/john-adams-and-the-rule-of-law/) . This principle is the bedrock of liberty, ensuring that law is a neutral, objective force [[67]](https://www.acslaw.org/inbrief/what-is-the-rule-of-law/)[[66]](https://allthingsliberty.com/2022/05/john-adams-and-the-rule-of-law/) . This Court has now replaced that simple, elegant principle with a complex and dangerous framework of tiers and presumptions [[7]](https://pennlawreview.com/2026/03/06/power-and-immunity-in-youngstown-and-trump-v-united-states/) . To declare that a President is absolutely immune for some acts [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States) , presumptively immune for others [[4]](https://ballotpedia.org/Trump_v._United_States) , and accountable for a third category is to make the law's power dependent not on its own majesty, but on the nature of the alleged crime. Even when I, facing a quasi-war, signed the Alien and Sedition Acts, those were laws passed by Congress to meet a perceived national security crisis, not a judicial invention of immunity [[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_and_Sedition_Acts)[[69]](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/adams-alien-and-seditions-act/)[[43]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1P4ZpJ5pi4t0L9xO162vrx6Qw2tLbj1lSJ7-b1IISsVs)[[70]](https://pandcvol1redesign.s3.amazonaws.com/P%26C/John+Adams+and+the+Alien+and+Sedition+Act/Handout+A+John+Adams+and+the+Alien+and+Sedition+Acts.pdf) . They were fiercely contested, and rightly so, but they did not place the President himself beyond the law's reach. This ruling does. We have exchanged the clarity of law for the arbitrary complexity of judicial tiers, and in so doing, we have lost the essence of a free government."

**Justification for Adams's Perspective:**

John Adams, a legal scholar and philosopher, was the nation's foremost champion of "a government of laws, and not of men" [[64]](https://constitutioncenter.org/education/classroom-resource-library/classroom/6.5-primary-source-john-adams-thoughts-on-government-1776)[[67]](https://www.acslaw.org/inbrief/what-is-the-rule-of-law/)[[71]](https://khattalyconsulting.com/en/analysis/a-government-of-laws-not-of-men)[[72]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1_FwuzQu16O29BRGt70sniQmJKVmWaxpPOlzVHnVG-qI&resourcekey=0-eLEHd9U8OkcVh8v4l3THdQ) . This was not a platitude but the central pillar of his political philosophy, and he would view the Court's ruling as a direct assault upon it [[73]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1ukglQ4CJHjCx_ZMK1a4E9LwGhIoh5WjbtdJ_cbXPonI&resourcekey=0-ezK1PNJvVgTa55IdhY327Q)[[74]](https://mail.google.com/mail/?extsrc=sync&client=h&plid=ACUX6DPbQ1uVcwU6pyp-Zk7fs_NHcMYbQdQWvy8&mid=19f00d0326044874)[[75]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1v529QtxpMCEwJ4C5REzqO63BUQlFWFDU)[[76]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=11WSeCGQVz0givmyMOfuG4fSFYf6Ot4A3)[[77]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1IP4FCTUyDv8vxC4bBfIA2-N4QgOCEY_GAaYXRKA6bqc)[[78]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1ZrCNY52CwHAnKArxykDrTjcU9RSFraCR&resourcekey=0-lBBKRQaJUEwjbPugNOGh-Q) .

*   **Architect of "A Government of Laws":** Adams's most enduring contribution to this principle is the Massachusetts Constitution of 1780, which he almost single-handedly drafted [[79]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=10Cm9AHzyeJaoQCtcXsC4oqSxE-tRRLsJJEScSG2dGYg&resourcekey=0-Uf4tfrY1sT1TeCagg6C9GA)[[80]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1V74IjROeBfBsxIMJ7oJtasAigkrL0gCsbmAndvISlLI)[[81]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=10EoZv-EsDziMYJa1JQqQNuq3JXEqI1PM5YaCOgE9Idg)[[82]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1lK5krI4tFpyhzf_ZaQhuwhwQmfHQfj65xdCQ78lJi64&resourcekey=0-ucyeGD-7YNiXxh_k-k2Y7w)[[83]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1V0G-cQ2BeZX0V-2XGy4SzgkYSaTNXk8MXQu-pznFWBM)[[84]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=17tgj9xOLyhM7TLn-FnQMyf4GD2MIyPeE9551ssoJJgM)[[85]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=10pjKadbcTTLuQ1MqkBFVzQFCPVw3gmQe)[[86]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1pEjx5b7fW76PJLtMVmQHf9A9nr1BRzcswxF7i0l8WSI)[[87]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1V_So2xpoDI88-r8drOhiQFW98wolOkK1QEIsceBAEgU) . In Article XXX of its Declaration of Rights, he explicitly separated the legislative, executive, and judicial branches "to the end it may be a government of laws and not of men" [[65]](https://www.mass.gov/guides/john-adams-the-massachusetts-constitution)[[66]](https://allthingsliberty.com/2022/05/john-adams-and-the-rule-of-law/) . This structure was designed to prevent the arbitrary exercise of power and ensure all officials were bound by the same established laws [[88]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/activities/thematic-essays-john-adams-1735-1826/) . The Court's three-tiered framework [[7]](https://pennlawreview.com/2026/03/06/power-and-immunity-in-youngstown-and-trump-v-united-states/)  would be, in his view, the very definition of a government of men, where judges—not the law itself—make the ultimate determination of accountability.

*   **A Principle Tested by Crisis:** Adams's own presidency severely tested this principle [[43]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1P4ZpJ5pi4t0L9xO162vrx6Qw2tLbj1lSJ7-b1IISsVs) . During the Quasi-War with France, he signed the Alien and Sedition Acts of 1798, which granted the executive broad powers to deport non-citizens and criminalized criticism of the government [[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_and_Sedition_Acts)[[89]](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/alien-and-sedition-acts) . Adams and his Federalist supporters justified these as necessary "war measures" to protect national security from French influence and domestic subversion [[89]](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/alien-and-sedition-acts)[[90]](https://www.americanacorner.com/blog/alien-and-sedition-acts)[[91]](https://millercenter.org/president/john-adams/key-events)[[69]](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/adams-alien-and-seditions-act/) . However, the acts were widely condemned as unconstitutional and were used to prosecute political opponents [[92]](https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/historic-document-library/detail/the-alien-and-sedition-acts-1798)[[93]](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskHistorians/comments/27sqz2/how_did_the_founding_fathers_justify_passing_the/)[[94]](https://millercenter.org/president/adams/domestic-affairs) . This episode demonstrates that even Adams, when faced with perceived threats, prioritized government authority in a way that compromised his core philosophy [[95]](https://ojs.unito.it/index.php/rsajournal/article/download/8779/7323/28503)[[96]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/activities/handout-a-john-adams-and-the-alien-and-sedition-acts/) . Yet, crucially, these were acts of Congress, and Adams's enforcement of them was a political decision with immense political consequences, not an action shielded by a pre-existing legal immunity. He would see a profound difference between making a controversial executive decision under a law passed by the legislature and being judicially declared immune from the law for it.

*   **A Profile in Legal Courage:** Adams's commitment to the universal supremacy of law was most vividly demonstrated in his defense of the British soldiers after the Boston Massacre [[97]](https://stories.nd.edu/stories/the-rule-of-law-prevails/) . Despite intense public hatred, he took the case because he believed the law must be applied impartially to all [[98]](https://www.samsonhistorical.com/blogs/reliving-history/john-adams-defense)[[97]](https://stories.nd.edu/stories/the-rule-of-law-prevails/)[[99]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1WmI8xEBRiTbT-krXS-OpPUBapf_6-E1SxRUZ7lx9Qmg&resourcekey=0-yyE0LZjvYc9dlFLbt5vJ4g)[[100]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1w60EfX5Cm-jDCNvsiqjSZCnIK2haLzqJQ3sUqW2ho2o) . He famously argued that the law must be "deaf as an adder to the clamours of the populace" [[101]](https://historicalsnapshots.substack.com/p/john-adams-defends-british-troops-d11)[[97]](https://stories.nd.edu/stories/the-rule-of-law-prevails/)[[102]](https://mail.google.com/mail/?extsrc=sync&client=h&plid=ACUX6DOt0A1rjTZnBjF_Jyih_xHILqIS-opgz3M&mid=19dfcc2e1f4cfd74) . For Adams, a ruling that creates special legal categories for a president violates this core belief in the equal and universal application of the law.

### James Madison: The Shattering of the Constitutional Architecture

"If men were angels, no government would be necessary [[103]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-51/)[[104]](https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/historic-document-library/detail/james-madison-federalist-no-51-1788) . This ruling, however, proceeds from the naive assumption that a President, shielded by layers of immunity, will remain virtuous. The entire structure of our Constitution is a deliberate defense against the predictable 'encroaching spirit of power' [[105]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed48.asp)[[106]](https://ballotpedia.org/Federalist_No._48_by_James_Madison_(1788)) . We designed a system where 'ambition must be made to counteract ambition,' giving each department the 'constitutional means' to resist the others [[103]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-51/)[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._51) . To grant the executive absolute immunity for 'core' powers [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States)[[7]](https://pennlawreview.com/2026/03/06/power-and-immunity-in-youngstown-and-trump-v-united-states/)  and a high wall of 'presumptive' immunity for other official acts [[4]](https://ballotpedia.org/Trump_v._United_States)[[8]](https://www.law.virginia.edu/node/2202121)  is to strip the judiciary of its constitutional means to check the most dangerous branch. It dismantles this carefully constructed edifice, rendering our 'parchment barriers' useless [[106]](https://ballotpedia.org/Federalist_No._48_by_James_Madison_(1788))[[108]](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/federalist-no-48-2/) . The President, now unburdened by the full force of criminal law for his official conduct, is no longer one of three co-equal branches, but a power superior to the law itself in certain domains. This is not a mere interpretation of our design; it is its subversion, creating the very 'tyranny' of concentrated power I warned against [[109]](https://civics.asu.edu/node/621)[[110]](https://ballotpedia.org/Federalist_No._47_by_James_Madison_(1788)) ."

**Justification for Madison's Perspective:**

As the primary architect of the Constitution and a key author of the Federalist Papers, James Madison's reaction would be one of profound structural horror [[103]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-51/) . His political philosophy was based on a realistic view of human nature and the need for robust checks and balances to prevent the "accumulation of all powers...in the same hands," which he called "the very definition of tyranny" [[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._47)[[109]](https://civics.asu.edu/node/621) .

*   **A System Built for Fallible Humans:** Madison famously wrote, "If men were angels, no government would be necessary" [[103]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-51/)[[104]](https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/historic-document-library/detail/james-madison-federalist-no-51-1788) . His entire constitutional framework was designed to control the abuses of government by fallible leaders [[103]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-51/) . During the Constitutional Convention, he argued that impeachment was "indispensable" to defend the community against the "incapacity, negligence or perfidy of the chief Magistrate" [[112]](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/impeachment/impeachment-1780s)[[113]](https://www.cato.org/white-paper/indispensable-remedy-broad-scope-constitutions-impeachment-power) . He specifically rejected the idea that simply waiting for the next election was a sufficient check, warning that a president could "pervert his administration into a scheme of peculation or oppression" or "betray his trust to foreign powers" in the interim [[114]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/debates_720.asp)[[112]](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/impeachment/impeachment-1780s)[[115]](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/inside-founding-fathers-debate-over-what-constituted-impeachable-offense-180965083/) .

*   **Ambition Counteracting Ambition:** His solution, articulated in Federalist No. 51, was to create a self-regulating system where "ambition must be made to counteract ambition" [[103]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-51/)[[116]](https://fiveable.me/ap-gov/key-terms/federalist-no51) . This meant giving each branch the "necessary constitutional means and personal motives to resist encroachments of the others" [[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._51) . The judiciary's power to prosecute is a critical "constitutional means" of checking the executive, a process he and other framers understood would follow a political removal via impeachment [[117]](https://www.jurist.org/commentary/2020/02/the-framers-views-on-impeachment/)[[118]](https://www.socialscience.international/federalist-papers-impeachment)[[112]](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/impeachment/impeachment-1780s) .

*   **Dismantling the Checks:** Madison would see the Court's ruling as a catastrophic failure of this design. He warned in Federalist No. 48 that mere "parchment barriers" in a constitution are insufficient to prevent the "encroaching spirit of power" [[105]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed48.asp)[[106]](https://ballotpedia.org/Federalist_No._48_by_James_Madison_(1788)) . By creating categories of immunity, the Court has unilaterally disarmed the judiciary, removing one of its most essential checks on the executive. This upsets the delicate balance he engineered, allowing the executive's "encroaching nature" to expand without the intended resistance [[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._48) . He would argue that this decision doesn't interpret the Constitution; it actively dismantles a core component of its "double security" for liberty [[116]](https://fiveable.me/ap-gov/key-terms/federalist-no51)[[104]](https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/historic-document-library/detail/james-madison-federalist-no-51-1788) .

### Alexander Hamilton: An Energetic Executive, Not an Imperial One

"I have always argued for an 'energy in the Executive' as essential to good government and the 'security of liberty' [[120]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-70/)[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._70) . But I have never argued for an executive who stands above the law he is sworn to 'take Care be faithfully executed.' Energy is not a synonym for impunity. A vigorous executive is necessary for the 'steady administration of the laws,' as I demonstrated when confronting the Whiskey Rebellion, not for their violation [[120]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-70/)[[122]](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/duel-whiskey-rebellion/) . Indeed, I argued that a single executive could be watched 'more narrowly' and held responsible [[123]](https://publius.academy/what-is-federalist-70-about)[[120]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-70/)[[124]](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/the-federalist/) . This ruling contorts that principle into a license for unaccountability. I explicitly contrasted our President—who is liable to impeachment, removal, and 'afterwards...prosecution and punishment in the ordinary course of law'—with the British king, whose person is 'sacred and inviolable' [[125]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed69.asp)[[126]](https://talkingpointsmemo.com/edblog/yes-hamilton-thought-john-roberts-was-full-of-it-too)[[127]](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/federalist-no-69-the-real-character-the-executive) . By granting any form of immunity for official acts, the Court has blurred this vital distinction, creating a President who is not energetic, but imperial; not a guardian of the law, but its master in certain realms. This is a grave distortion."

**Justification for Hamilton's Perspective:**

Alexander Hamilton, in the Federalist Papers, was the foremost champion of a strong, "energetic" executive, but his argument was always framed by accountability [[120]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-70/)[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._70)[[128]](https://ashbrook.org/viewpoint/alexander-hamilton-and-the-federalist/)[[123]](https://publius.academy/what-is-federalist-70-about)[[129]](https://hub.edubirdie.com/examples/hamiltons-defense-of-a-single-executive-leader/) . He would be appalled by the idea that this energy could be used to place entire categories of presidential action beyond the reach of criminal law.

*   **Energy Requires Accountability:** In Federalist No. 70, Hamilton argued that "Energy in the Executive is a leading character in the definition of good government," essential for national defense and the "steady administration of the laws" [[120]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-70/)[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._70) . He advocated for a single president not to create an unaccountable ruler, but because a unitary executive could be watched "more narrowly" by the people and could not "conceal faults, and destroy responsibility" as a council might [[130]](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/the-federalist-papers/federalist-no-70)[[123]](https://publius.academy/what-is-federalist-70-about)[[131]](https://civicsinayear.buzzsprout.com/2501662/episodes/17730788-hamilton-s-vision-understanding-executive-authority-in-federalist-no-70)[[124]](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/the-federalist/) .

*   **Energy in Service of the Law:** Hamilton's philosophy was put into practice during the Whiskey Rebellion of 1794 [[29]](https://www.exploros.com/summary/The-Whiskey-Rebellion-Testing-Federal-Authority-2)[[132]](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/the-disagreeable-crisis-in-the-western-counties-report-on-the-whiskey-rebellion/)[[133]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1186on0d4vPycWNC-OVDH968QDCE2M7xOLgHyaKX3E_I) . When Pennsylvania farmers violently resisted a federal tax, Hamilton saw it as a direct challenge to the authority of the new government [[134]](https://sentinelroom.com/article/the-whiskey-rebellion)[[122]](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/duel-whiskey-rebellion/)[[135]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1dfjg0Q8tQBUcZdOYhVFJm-PF9zD2M9KbObDKQ_zRMfs) . He argued for a decisive, forceful response to demonstrate that federal law was supreme and binding on all citizens [[122]](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/duel-whiskey-rebellion/)[[136]](https://hamilton.gilderlehrman.org/supporting-document/hamilton-asks-militia-quell-whiskey-rebellion-1794)[[137]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1WKUKvp1ddr9FbaxesHFpv9wM1uF14crqpyPl39pWLbU&resourcekey=0-F2kGnIR6AtmXcoPTorYNfg) . His actions, in urging President Washington to lead a militia to suppress the rebellion, showed that he believed executive energy was a tool to *enforce* the law and ensure all citizens remained subordinate to it, not a means for the executive to rise above it [[31]](https://www.ttb.gov/public-information/special-feature)[[138]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1-yzvheSUt9lsMgFzLErRxG28XunWVVcXCmfCq4p1S9Y) .

*   **The Explicit Rejection of Royal Impunity:** Hamilton's most direct refutation of presidential immunity comes from the clear, two-step process of accountability he outlined. In Federalist No. 65, he defined impeachment as a "POLITICAL" process for abuses of public trust [[139]](https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/what-the-founders-thought-about-impeachment-and-the-president)[[140]](https://academic.brooklyn.cuny.edu/history/johnson/fedimpeachment.htm) . Then, in Federalist No. 69, to soothe fears of an "elective monarchy," he meticulously contrasted the American president with the King of Great Britain [[141]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._69)[[142]](https://www.gradesaver.com/the-federalist-papers/study-guide/summary-essay-69) . He stated that unlike the king, whose person is "sacred and inviolable," the President is liable to be impeached, tried, and removed from office, and "would afterwards be liable to prosecution and punishment in the ordinary course of law" [[125]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed69.asp)[[126]](https://talkingpointsmemo.com/edblog/yes-hamilton-thought-john-roberts-was-full-of-it-too)[[143]](https://criticalskillsblog.com/2024/08/11/federalist-paper-69-the-real-character-of-the-executive/)[[35]](https://journals.law.harvard.edu/jlpp/the-presidential-immunity-decision-robert-delahunty-john-yoo/) . This statement, echoed by other proponents of the Constitution, demonstrates that prosecution after office was a foundational element of presidential accountability from the outset [[112]](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/impeachment/impeachment-1780s) .

### Executive Summary

The Supreme Court's 2024 ruling in *Trump v. United States* would be viewed by the Founding Fathers as a grave and dangerous error, even if it stopped short of granting total immunity. Their critiques would focus on the specifics of the new legal framework and its violation of the core principles of accountability they established.

*   **George Washington** would see the grant of "absolute immunity" for core powers [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_v._United_States)[[7]](https://pennlawreview.com/2026/03/06/power-and-immunity-in-youngstown-and-trump-v-united-states/)  as a step toward the monarchy he disdained [[19]](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/two-georges/about-this-exhibition/president-and-king/)[[11]](https://www.quora.com/Did-George-Washington-see-himself-as-President-or-King-and-which-did-he-prefer-for-the-future-of-America) . He would view it as a betrayal of his life's work to establish a president who is a servant of the people and bound by the Constitution, which he held as "sacredly obligatory upon all" [[17]](https://www.nationalreview.com/corner/george-washington-on-respecting-the-constitution-as-written/)[[18]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/washing.asp) .
*   **Thomas Jefferson** would view the ruling as the predictable work of the "despotic branch" he always feared—the judiciary [[32]](https://www.facebook.com/groups/339317480411211/posts/1838450463831231/) . He would point to his aggressive prosecution of Aaron Burr as proof of his belief that no official is above the law, and argue that the Court has ignored the fundamental republican principle that a president acting beyond the law must still "throw himself on the justice of his country" [[39]](https://www.americanyawp.com/text/07-the-early-republic/)[[41]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1QoQPtsEWL1-o3Yi430myawIFC3muPEXGKg6ElbMCs8U)[[38]](https://www.britannica.com/today-in-history/May-22-Aaron-Burrs-Treason-Trial-Begins)[[37]](https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/the-great-trial-that-tested-the-constitutions-treason-clause) .
*   **John Adams** would decry the multi-tiered framework [[7]](https://pennlawreview.com/2026/03/06/power-and-immunity-in-youngstown-and-trump-v-united-states/)  as the death of his most cherished principle: "a government of laws, and not of men" [[65]](https://www.mass.gov/guides/john-adams-the-massachusetts-constitution)[[66]](https://allthingsliberty.com/2022/05/john-adams-and-the-rule-of-law/) . He would argue it replaces the universal supremacy of law with arbitrary judicial judgment, noting that even his own controversial enforcement of the Alien and Sedition Acts was authorized by Congress, not protected by a pre-existing legal doctrine of immunity [[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_and_Sedition_Acts)[[43]](https://drive.google.com/a/google.com/open?id=1P4ZpJ5pi4t0L9xO162vrx6Qw2tLbj1lSJ7-b1IISsVs)[[91]](https://millercenter.org/president/john-adams/key-events) .
*   **James Madison** would see the ruling as a structural collapse of his system where "ambition must be made to counteract ambition" [[103]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-51/)[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_No._51) . By removing the judiciary's "constitutional means" to check the executive, the Court has dangerously weakened the bulwarks against the "encroaching spirit of power," a danger he explicitly warned of at the Constitutional Convention [[105]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed48.asp)[[106]](https://ballotpedia.org/Federalist_No._48_by_James_Madison_(1788))[[114]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/debates_720.asp)[[113]](https://www.cato.org/white-paper/indispensable-remedy-broad-scope-constitutions-impeachment-power) .
*   **Alexander Hamilton**, the advocate for a strong executive, would be horrified to see his call for "energy" twisted into a justification for impunity [[120]](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/primary-sources/federalist-no-70/) . He would point directly to his writings in Federalist No. 69, where he explicitly stated that a president, unlike a king, would be liable to "prosecution and punishment in the ordinary course of law" after removal from office [[125]](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed69.asp)[[126]](https://talkingpointsmemo.com/edblog/yes-hamilton-thought-john-roberts-was-full-of-it-too)[[127]](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/federalist-no-69-the-real-character-the-executive) .

In unison, they would likely agree with the dissenting justices [[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939)[[4]](https://ballotpedia.org/Trump_v._United_States)[[144]](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2024/07/01/sotomayor-jackson-trump-immunity-dissent/)[[145]](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2024/07/sotomayor-jackson-blistering-dissents-trump-immunity-case/) : by creating special legal protections for the president, the Court has damaged the fundamental principle that no person in the republic is above the law, risking the very foundation of the nation they built [[6]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/read-the-full-supreme-court-decision-on-trump-and-presidential-immunity)[[3]](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2023/23-939) .
